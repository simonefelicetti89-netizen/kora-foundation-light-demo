# KORA Product Architecture, Tiering & Pricing Logic

*Status: Approved — Strong First Version*

---

## 1. Product Vision

KORA is not a dashboard. It is not a report generator. It is not a welfare management system.

KORA is a multi-tier Impact Intelligence Platform — an infrastructure layer that transforms verified human actions inside organizations into structured impact data, strategic indices, financial governance intelligence, and evidence-grade output.

The product evolves across four commercial tiers — Foundation Light, Foundation, Governance, Certified — plus a future ecosystem layer. Each tier delivers a complete, standalone value proposition. Each tier also serves as the entry ramp to the next.

The commercial logic is progressive:

- A company starts with Foundation Light to understand where it stands.
- It moves to Foundation to track and improve over time.
- It adds Governance to control spending and decisions.
- It reaches Certified to prove and communicate its impact externally.

The ecosystem layer — worker app, partner portal, advisor network, KORA Link, marketplace, territorial intelligence — lives above the four tiers and activates when the company is ready to expand from intelligence into real-time action infrastructure.

This is not a ladder companies are forced to climb. Each tier can be a permanent state. But the architecture must be designed so that moving up is natural, technically seamless and commercially obvious.

## 2. Product Tiers

---

### TIER 1 — KORA Foundation Light

**Target customer**
Mid-to-large companies (500–5,000 employees) that already invest in welfare, training or ESG programs and want to understand what that investment actually produced. HR directors, People & Culture leads, Sustainability managers. Companies preparing for ESG audits or investor reporting who need credible evidence. Companies that know something is broken in their people program but cannot diagnose what.

**Core promise**
"We take what you already have and show you what it means."

No new infrastructure. No worker app. No partner integrations. A company provides its existing data — CSV exports, HR records, welfare reports, training logs, ESG declarations — and KORA returns a structured picture of human impact across the five pillars, with a prototype KORA Index and a first executive report.

**Key features**
- One-time or annual data upload (CSV, Excel, structured exports)
- Automated action classification into LIFE, GROWTH, CONNECTION, IMPACT, LEGACY
- Confidence score per data source (how verified, how complete)
- Prototype KORA Index
- Pillar balance visualization
- Activation rate analysis (employees active vs enrolled vs excluded)
- Basic financial governance (budget declared vs impact generated)
- Executive dashboard
- Exportable HR/ESG report snapshot

**What problem it solves**
The company has been investing in people for years. It has data spread across twelve systems. Nobody can answer the question: what human value did we actually generate? Foundation Light answers that question for the first time.

**Why a company buys it**
- Preparing for ESG/sustainability reporting and needs evidence, not declarations
- Board or investor pressure to demonstrate people investment ROI
- Internal audit: the welfare budget is large but nobody knows if it works
- Competitive positioning: the company wants to claim human impact leadership
- Strategic reset: the HR director wants a defensible baseline before redesigning the program

**Possible pricing logic**
- One-time setup fee (analysis + report): €8,000–€25,000 depending on company size and data complexity
- Optional annual subscription for recurring ingestion and updated dashboard: €5,000–€15,000/year
- Per-employee band pricing at scale: €3–€8 per employee per year for large enterprises

**Data it needs**
- HR records (headcount, departments, job families)
- Welfare program records (services offered, participation data, provider names)
- Training records (courses, certifications, completions)
- ESG/people initiative declarations
- Budget allocation per program (optional but valuable)

**Output it produces**
- Prototype KORA Index (0–100 scale)
- Pillar balance radar/chart
- Activation rate by department or cohort
- Confidence score per pillar
- Financial efficiency ratio (budget vs impact)
- Executive summary report (PDF or web)
- Data quality assessment

### TIER 2 — KORA Foundation

**Target customer**
Companies that completed Foundation Light and want to track their program over time. Companies actively managing people programs who need recurring intelligence to improve decisions quarterly. HR and People teams with internal reporting responsibilities. Sustainability leads who must produce periodic people-impact evidence.

**Core promise**
"Continuous intelligence, not a one-time snapshot."

KORA Foundation transforms the one-time analysis of Foundation Light into a recurring intelligence system. Data flows in quarterly or monthly. The KORA Index evolves. Pillar trends become visible. Initiative portfolios can be compared. Decisions improve because the company can see whether actions are working.

**Key features**
- Recurring data ingestion (quarterly or monthly cycles)
- KORA Evolution tracking (how the Index changes over time)
- Improved KORA Index with longitudinal weighting
- Richer workforce intelligence (activation by segment, department, tenure)
- Initiative portfolio view (which programs generated what impact)
- Partner usage visibility (which providers are generating real activation)
- Pillar strategy view (where to invest next)
- Narrative report generation (quarterly people impact report)
- Baseline comparison (current period vs prior period vs first baseline)

**What problem it solves**
A one-time score is a diagnosis. A company needs continuous intelligence to manage. Foundation solves the management problem: it gives HR and People leadership a living system, not a static report.

**Why a company buys it**
- They bought Foundation Light, saw the diagnosis, and want to improve
- They have a quarterly board reporting cycle and need structured evidence each time
- They are redesigning their people program and want to measure whether changes work
- They want to compare departments or cohorts over time

**Possible pricing logic**
- Annual subscription: €18,000–€45,000/year depending on employee count and ingestion frequency
- Per-employee band: €8–€18 per employee per year
- Includes Foundation Light as baseline onboarding

**Output it produces**
- KORA Evolution timeline
- Quarterly people impact report
- Initiative ROI view
- Workforce activation heat map
- Pillar trend analysis
- Recommended rebalancing actions (manual, not AI)

### TIER 3 — KORA Governance

**Target customer**
Large companies (2,000+ employees) or companies with complex, multi-provider people programs where budget control, decision accountability and audit readiness matter. CFOs and Finance who need to control people-program spending. CHROs who must defend people investment decisions to the board. Legal and Compliance teams who need audit trails.

**Core promise**
"Turn your people program into a governable, auditable investment."

KORA Governance adds the full financial and decision control layer on top of Foundation. Budget allocation, cost per Impact Unit, policy rules, verification workflows, risk alerts and board-grade reporting live here.

**Key features**
- Budget allocation by pillar and initiative
- Spend vs impact reconciliation
- Cost per Impact Unit by pillar, provider, initiative
- Policy rules engine (minimum % in each pillar, maximum concentration constraints)
- Verification workflow (declared vs verified vs certified)
- Audit trail for all data inputs and score changes
- Risk alerts (budget depleted in one pillar while another has surplus; activation below threshold)
- Decision intelligence (structured recommendations based on current state)
- Advisor involvement layer
- Board/ESG/HR reporting package

**Possible pricing logic**
- Annual subscription: €40,000–€120,000/year depending on employee count and governance complexity
- Per-employee band: €15–€35 per employee per year
- Optional advisor fee layer
- Enterprise plan with custom SLA for companies with >5,000 employees

**Output it produces**
- Governance dashboard
- Budget vs impact reconciliation report
- Audit trail export
- Board reporting package
- Risk and alert log
- Decision summary

### TIER 4 — KORA Certified

**Target customer**
Companies that want to communicate their human impact externally with credibility — to investors, ESG rating agencies, talent markets, regulators, and the public. Companies competing on purpose and culture. Companies with ESG commitments that must be externally validated.

**Core promise**
"Prove it. Not just to yourself — to the world."

KORA Certified is the external validation layer. It is a methodology assessment, an external review process, and a credentialed output that a company can present to the market.

**Key features**
- Validated KORA Methodology assessment (annual)
- External advisor review of data sources, classification logic and scoring
- Benchmark comparison (anonymized peer comparison within KORA network)
- KORA Certified badge
- Methodology report (transparent explanation of how the score was produced)
- Verified people-impact evidence package
- Annual assessment cycle with delta analysis
- Public KORA Impact Profile (optional, company-controlled)

**Possible pricing logic**
- Annual certification fee: €15,000–€40,000 depending on company size
- Assessment fee (external advisor): €5,000–€20,000 per cycle
- Certification renewal: discounted if prior year assessment passes threshold
- Partner certification: separate fee

**Output it produces**
- KORA Certified status (annual)
- Certified badge and usage license
- Methodology transparency report
- Verified evidence package
- Public KORA Impact Profile (optional)
- Benchmark positioning (anonymized)

## 3. Module Map

Each module is a bounded capability within KORA. Modules belong to specific tiers.

**Impact Intelligence**
What it does: Classifies verified actions into the five pillars, calculates the KORA Index and sub-indicators, produces the core impact signal.
Tier: Foundation Light and above. Status: Core. Dependencies: Data Ingestion & Mapping.

**Data Ingestion & Mapping**
What it does: Receives raw company data, normalizes it, maps events to pillars, assigns confidence scores.
Tier: Foundation Light and above. Status: Core. Dependencies: None upstream; feeds Impact Intelligence.

**Financial Governance**
What it does: Tracks budget allocation, spend by pillar, cost per Impact Unit, partner efficiency, budget vs impact reconciliation.
Tier: Foundation Light (basic), Foundation (richer), Governance (full). Status: Core basic / Advanced full.

**Workforce Intelligence**
What it does: Produces anonymized, aggregated activation data by department, cohort, tenure, job family. Never exposes individual scores.
Tier: Foundation (basic), Governance (full segmentation). Status: Advanced.

**Pillar Strategy**
What it does: Identifies imbalances across pillars, highlights underfunded or overconcentrated areas, surfaces structured rebalancing suggestions.
Tier: Foundation and above. Status: Advanced.

**Reporting & Evidence**
What it does: Generates executive dashboards, narrative reports, board packages, ESG evidence exports, certified methodology reports.
Tier: All tiers (output format scales with tier). Status: Core at all tiers.

**Partner Network**
What it does: Manages the catalog of welfare, training, ESG and other approved partners. Tracks partner usage, activation rates and efficiency.
Tier: Foundation (visibility), Governance (management), Ecosystem (partner portal). Status: Advanced / Future.

**Advisor Layer**
What it does: Gives external KORA-authorized advisors controlled read/annotate access to a company's dashboard.
Tier: Governance (involvement), Certified (review). Status: Advanced.

**Certification Layer**
What it does: Manages the annual KORA Certified assessment cycle, external reviewer assignment, methodology validation, badge issuance.
Tier: Certified only. Status: Future.

**Territory Layer**
What it does: Aggregates anonymized impact data across companies in a geography or sector for territorial intelligence.
Tier: Future ecosystem. Status: Future.

**Worker Layer**
What it does: Individual worker-facing interface showing personal PIB, action history, available programs.
Tier: Future ecosystem. Status: Future.

**KORA Link Layer**
What it does: Physical or digital device/access point connecting workers to KORA actions in physical spaces.
Tier: Future ecosystem. Status: Future.

**Payments Layer**
What it does: Manages welfare budget distribution, partner payment settlement, marketplace transactions.
Tier: Future ecosystem. Status: Future.

**AI Insight Layer**
What it does: Predictive recommendations, anomaly detection, natural language report generation, smart rebalancing.
Tier: Future (partial in Governance as decision intelligence). Status: Future.

## 4. Pricing Model Options

**Option A — Setup Fee + Annual Subscription**
One-time setup fee covers onboarding, data ingestion and first analysis. Annual subscription covers recurring intelligence and support.
- Pros: predictable revenue; setup fee filters non-serious buyers; subscription creates retention
- Cons: setup fee creates friction in enterprise sales cycles

**Option B — Per Employee Pricing**
Annual fee banded by employee count.
- Pros: scales naturally with company size; familiar to HR buyers
- Cons: incentivizes undercounting; commoditizes KORA into "HR software per seat"

**Option C — Pilot/Assessment Fee**
Fixed-price engagement for the first analysis. Positioned as a strategic assessment.
- Pros: very low friction for first sale; clear deliverable; easy budget approval
- Cons: no recurring revenue; requires clear upgrade path

**Option D — Tiered Annual Subscription (No Setup Fee)**
Foundation Light at lower price, Foundation at mid-range, Governance at premium.
- Pros: lower friction; easier to trial
- Cons: no commitment signal at entry; harder to recover onboarding cost if churn is early

**Option E — Value-Based Pricing**
Price tied to the financial value KORA helps unlock — percentage of the welfare/training budget optimized.
- Pros: compelling ROI story; aligns incentives with company outcomes
- Cons: complex to define; hard to standardize; negotiation friction

**Option F — Certification Fee (Certified Tier)**
Separate annual fee for KORA Certified assessment, badge and public profile.
- Pros: clear product boundary; premium revenue without inflating operational tier pricing
- Cons: requires credible certification process and auditor network before selling

**Recommended commercial architecture:**
Foundation Light → one-time assessment fee (€8,000–€25,000)
Foundation → annual subscription per employee band (€18,000–€45,000/year)
Governance → annual subscription with enterprise pricing (€40,000–€120,000/year)
Certified → annual certification fee stacked on Governance (€15,000–€40,000/year)

## 5. First Build Strategy

**What to build first:** KORA Foundation Light, in its entirety. Nothing else.

The first build must be capable of:
1. Accepting structured data uploads from a company (CSV, Excel, standard export formats)
2. Classifying those actions into the five pillars with a defined taxonomy
3. Calculating a prototype KORA Index with a documented, explainable formula
4. Displaying pillar balance and basic financial governance
5. Generating an exportable executive report

**What to design conceptually now but build later:**
- The Foundation recurring data cycle (same architecture as Foundation Light, just scheduled)
- The Governance policy engine (built on top of the financial governance already in Foundation Light)
- The Certified assessment workflow (built on top of Governance data and audit trail)
- The workforce intelligence segmentation (same data, more granular slicing)

**The core principle:** Build the data model and classification engine to be extensible from day one. The core logic must be documented, version-controlled, and designed so that improving the formula in Foundation does not require rebuilding Foundation Light.

**What to avoid building now:** Any module requiring a worker-facing interface, real-time data, external API integrations, partner management, or payment processing.

## 6. Risk of Wrong Packaging

**If KORA is sold as welfare software:** Enters a market dominated by established platforms. KORA has no competitive advantage in managing providers or distributing welfare credits.

**If KORA is sold as an HR tool:** Competes with Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, and dozens of HRIS vendors. The category is wrong.

**If KORA is sold as ESG reporting software:** Limits the buyer to the sustainability team and removes the HR and Finance stakeholders who are the real decision-makers.

**If KORA is sold as an engagement app:** Evaluated against Peakon, Glint, Culture Amp. KORA's differentiation — verified actions, not sentiment — disappears.

**The correct framing:** KORA is an Impact Intelligence Platform. The category KORA should own is one it defines: Human Impact Intelligence.

## 7. Upgrade Path

**Foundation Light → Foundation**
Trigger: the company received the one-time assessment and wants to track improvement over time. The need shifts from "what is our situation?" to "is our program working?". Migration effort: minimal.

**Foundation → Governance**
Trigger: program complexity grows (more providers, larger budget, multi-country operations) or accountability pressure increases (board, investor, ESG regulation). Migration effort: low — same data infrastructure, additional policy engine.

**Governance → Certified**
Trigger: the company wants to communicate impact externally with credibility. Migration effort: process-level (external advisor engagement), not technical.

## 8. Technical Implication

No code yet. These modularity principles must govern the architecture from day one.

**The core engine is shared across all tiers.** Classification logic, Impact Unit calculation and KORA Index formula are not tier-specific. All tiers read from the same engine.

**Tiers are access and feature layers, not separate products.** The data model must support all future modules from day one, even if most fields are empty or locked in early tiers.

**The data model must support future sources.** Foundation Light accepts flat files. Foundation may add scheduled ingestion. Governance may add API connections. The ingestion layer must be an abstraction.

**Verification is a field, not an afterthought.** Every action must carry a verification status from the beginning: declared, evidenced, or certified.

**Privacy is architecture, not policy.** Aggregation thresholds, anonymization rules and access controls must be built into the data model before the first dashboard is drawn.

**The KORA Index formula must be versioned.** Companies must be able to see how their score was calculated, which formula version was used, and how formula changes affect historical scores.

## 9. Founder Decision Points

- **First ICP:** Who is the very first paying customer? Size, sector, geography?
- **First paid pilot offer:** What exactly does a company get? One number, one deliverable, one timeline.
- **First module:** Data ingestion and mapping, or KORA Index engine? Determines build sequence.
- **Pricing range:** Mid-market SaaS (€5,000–€20,000/year) or premium strategic assessment (€20,000–€80,000/year)?
- **Data sources accepted:** Generic CSV only, or specific platform exports?
- **Algorithm transparency:** Full, partial, or summary?
- **Privacy thresholds:** Minimum group size for segment analytics?
- **Reporting promise:** PDF, web, interactive dashboard, slide deck?
- **Certification timeline:** When does KORA plan to launch the Certified tier?

---

## 10. Recommendation

**Sell strategic intelligence, not software.**

The first commercial offer should be a fixed-scope engagement (60–90 days) in which KORA takes a company's existing data, produces the first structured KORA analysis, and delivers an executive-grade people impact report. Price: €10,000–€20,000 as a fixed engagement fee, converting naturally into a Foundation annual subscription.

The full commercial architecture — Foundation, Governance, Certified, ecosystem — should be visible in all commercial conversations from day one. The first sale is not the end of the commercial relationship; it is the proof of concept that unlocks the subscription conversation.

Position KORA as the category leader in Human Impact Intelligence from the first engagement. The entry price is not the value; the category is the value.
