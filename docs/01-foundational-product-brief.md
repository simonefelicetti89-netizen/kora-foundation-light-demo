# KORA — Foundational Product Brief

*Status: Approved*

---

## 1. One-Sentence Definition

KORA is an Impact Intelligence Platform that transforms real-world human actions inside organizations into structured, verified and governable impact data.

---

## 2. Expanded Definition

KORA is the infrastructure layer that sits between what companies invest in people and what that investment actually produces.

Companies today spend significant budgets on welfare, training, wellbeing, safety, social initiatives, mentoring, ESG programs and culture-building. These investments are real. But they are managed through separate providers, separate tools, separate budgets, and separate reports. No single system reads all of it together and answers the fundamental question: what human value was actually created?

KORA does that.

It ingests data from existing systems — HR platforms, welfare providers, training platforms, ESG records, people initiatives — and transforms that heterogeneous data into a coherent intelligence framework organized across five pillars. The output is not a dashboard of activity. It is a structured, verifiable picture of human impact: where it is strong, where it is weak, where it is missing, and what to do next.

KORA is designed for companies that want to move beyond reporting activity and start governing human value.

---

## 3. The Core Problem KORA Solves

Organizations invest in people without being able to measure the return.

They can track spending. They can count events. They can generate activity reports. But they cannot answer:

- Are people actually activating, or just enrolled?
- Are initiatives generating measurable impact or consuming budget without effect?
- Is the company over-investing in one dimension and neglecting others?
- Are wellbeing, learning, social contribution and knowledge transfer connected or fragmented?
- Is impact distributed across the workforce or concentrated in a few segments?
- Are the actions being counted verified or just declared?
- Is the company building long-term human capital or only short-term activity?
- What is the real return on people-related spending?
- Can human contribution become measurable, comparable and governable over time?

The problem is structural. It is not a dashboarding problem. It is not a reporting problem. It is an intelligence infrastructure problem. No tool currently connects the dots between budget, action, impact and governance into a single coherent framework.

---

## 4. Why Existing Tools Do Not Solve This

**Welfare platforms** manage access to benefits and services. They track what was offered and sometimes what was used. They do not classify actions, calculate impact, or produce intelligence. They optimize for provider coverage, not human value.

**HR tools (HRIS)** manage employment records, contracts, org structure, performance cycles and payroll. They record events but do not interpret them as human impact. Impact is not their domain.

**ESG reporting software** aggregates declared data for compliance and investor reporting. It is oriented toward external communication, not internal intelligence. It does not validate activation or distinguish quality from quantity.

**LMS and training platforms** track course completions and certifications. They do not connect learning to wellbeing, social contribution or long-term organizational value. They operate in isolation.

**Employee engagement apps** measure sentiment, pulse and satisfaction. They capture perception, not verified action. Perception is valuable but it is not impact evidence.

None of these tools are wrong. They are excellent at what they do. But they were not designed to answer the question KORA is built to answer: what human value did this organization actually generate?

KORA is not a replacement for any of them. It is the intelligence layer that reads across all of them.

## 5. The Five-Pillar Model

The five pillars are the grammar of KORA. They are not decorative categories. They are the classification system that translates heterogeneous actions into a common organizational language.

**LIFE**
Health, wellbeing, prevention, psychological support, nutrition, physical activity, safety-related wellbeing, quality of life. Actions that help people live and work better.

**GROWTH**
Learning, skills development, certifications, digital upskilling, professional evolution, personal development. Actions that build human capital.

**CONNECTION**
Mentoring, peer support, collaboration, internal communities, cross-functional engagement, team cohesion, relational quality inside the organization. Actions that strengthen the relational fabric.

**IMPACT**
Volunteering, social projects, community support, environmental initiatives, school orientation, territorial contribution. Verified value generated beyond the individual and beyond the company.

**LEGACY**
Knowledge transfer, senior-junior mentorship, organizational memory, durable practices, cultural continuity. Actions that produce lasting value beyond the moment they occur.

Every action ingested by KORA is mapped to one or more of these pillars. The relative strength, balance and quality across the five pillars is the primary signal KORA produces.

---

## 6. Actions, Activity, Impact and Governance — The Four Layers

These four concepts are distinct. Confusing them is the most dangerous mistake KORA must avoid.

**INPUT**
What the company makes available: budget allocated, welfare funds, training spend, partner costs, programs offered, services available. Input is a resource, not a result.

**ACTIVITY**
What actually happens: events, sessions, completions, check-ins, participations, usage records. Activity measures that something occurred. It does not measure whether it mattered.

**IMPACT**
What the activity produced: verified actions mapped to pillars, Impact Units, activation rates, pillar balance, continuity, distribution across the workforce, verified externality. Impact is the real output of KORA. It requires classification, verification and a coherent scoring model.

**GOVERNANCE**
The decision layer: budget control, cost per outcome, data quality scores, verification levels, audit trail, confidence score, ESG and HR reporting readiness. Governance helps leadership act on what they learn.

A company can have high INPUT and low IMPACT. A company can have high ACTIVITY and low IMPACT. A company can have high IMPACT with limited INPUT. These distinctions are exactly what KORA must make visible.

---

## 7. The Role of Financial Governance

Financial governance is a decision support layer, not the impact model itself.

KORA must never confuse money spent with human value created. A large welfare budget is not the same as high impact. Many available partners is not the same as real activation. High participation counts are not the same as quality impact.

The financial governance layer answers different questions:

- How much budget has been allocated vs used?
- Which pillars absorbed the most spending?
- Which initiatives generated the most verified impact per euro spent?
- What is the cost per Impact Unit?
- Which partners are efficient? Which are not?
- Which areas are underfunded or overfunded relative to their impact output?
- Is the company spending without activation? Activating with minimal budget?
- Where should the next allocation go?

This layer does not determine the KORA Index. It informs the decisions that should follow from understanding the KORA Index. Financial governance is the bridge between intelligence and action.

## 8. The First Sellable Product: KORA Foundation Light

KORA Foundation Light is the MVP. It is the first product a company can buy or pilot today.

**Goal:** Take what a company already has — HR records, welfare data, training exports, ESG reports, people initiative logs — and transform it into a first layer of structured impact intelligence. No new infrastructure required. No worker app. No partner integration. No real-time data. Just existing data, intelligently interpreted.

**What Foundation Light delivers:**

- Data ingestion from existing company sources (CSV, structured exports, HR/welfare/training/ESG data)
- Automated classification of actions into the five KORA pillars
- Confidence scoring on data quality and verification level
- Prototype KORA Index calculation
- Pillar balance visualization
- Activation and participation analysis
- Financial governance summary (budget vs. impact)
- Executive dashboard for leadership
- Exportable HR/ESG-ready report snapshot

**What Foundation Light must feel like:**

It must feel like an executive intelligence cockpit, not an admin import panel. It must feel strategic and premium. The quality of the framing, the precision of the indicators, and the clarity of the output must signal that this is a new category of tool — not another HR dashboard.

## 9. First Company-Side Platform Areas (Concept Only — No Screens Yet)

These are the logical areas the company-side platform must cover. No UI design yet. No component definitions. This is only structural orientation.

**Impact Intelligence Center**
Where the company sees its KORA Index, pillar balance, activation rate, verification level and quality indicators. The core of what KORA produces.

**Data Ingestion & Mapping**
Where HR, welfare, training, ESG and initiative data is imported and classified. The operational entry point.

**Financial Governance View**
Where budget, cost per impact, pillar spend and partner efficiency are visible. The decision support layer.

**Workforce Intelligence**
Where anonymized, aggregated insight into workforce activation is shown — by segment, by department, by cohort. Individual data is never exposed.

**Report & Evidence Export**
Where the company generates its HR and ESG-ready output. The deliverable that justifies the investment.

## 10. What Must NOT Be Built Yet

The following are future phases. They must not be designed, scaffolded, or implied in the Foundation Light build.

- Worker-facing app or individual dashboard
- Partner portal or partner onboarding
- Advisor ecosystem
- Marketplace or benefit selection
- Payments or budget management
- KORA Link hardware
- Real-time data streams or integrations
- Territorial intelligence or benchmarking
- AI recommendations or automation engine
- Public impact status or certification layer
- Advanced scoring models beyond the prototype KORA Index
- LMS or learning module
- Full ESG compliance module

Every one of these features may be built. None of them belong in the first 90 days.

## 11. Main Risks If We Build KORA Incorrectly

**Flattening the concept into existing categories.**
If KORA feels like a welfare dashboard, an ESG reporter, or an HR admin tool, it will compete in crowded markets on weak ground. The positioning must be protected from day one.

**Confusing budget with impact.**
If financial data drives the KORA Index, the model becomes meaningless. A rich company with low activation would score high. A lean company with deep engagement would score low. The causal chain must remain: action → impact, not spend → score.

**Overbuilding before validating the core.**
If the team spends months building partner integrations, worker apps and marketplace infrastructure before a single company can see a coherent KORA Index, the product has no commercial value. The first sale must be possible with Foundation Light alone.

**Exposing individual employee data.**
If companies can see individual scores, KORA becomes a surveillance tool. This destroys trust, creates legal risk, and contradicts the platform's purpose. All individual data must be anonymized and aggregated.

**Treating verification as optional.**
If unverified actions count the same as verified actions, the KORA Index is not credible. The confidence and verification layer must be built from the beginning, even if imperfect.

**Building screens before building logic.**
If the UI is designed before the impact model is clear, the interface will drive the product instead of the intelligence. Logic must lead design.

**Overcomplicating the scoring model.**
If the KORA Index requires too many parameters to explain, it will not be trusted or sold. The model must be simple enough to describe in one conversation, rigorous enough to withstand a CTO's questions.

---

## 12. Principles That Must Guide Every Decision

**Clarity before completeness.** A partial feature that is clearly understood is more valuable than a full feature that is not.

**Impact before activity.** Always distinguish what happened from what it produced.

**Verification before quantity.** A smaller number of verified actions is worth more than a large number of unverified ones.

**Balance before maximum.** A company scoring high on one pillar at the expense of others is not a success. The model must reward balance.

**Aggregation before exposure.** Individual data must always be protected. The unit of analysis at the company level is the workforce, not the employee.

**Governance before automation.** Human decisions informed by intelligence are the goal. Automation comes later, when the intelligence layer is trusted.

**Premium positioning before feature volume.** KORA competes on depth and framing, not on the number of features. Each element must be correct and premium, not simply present.

**90-day sellability before long-term completeness.** Every build decision must be tested against one question: does this make Foundation Light more sellable to a company in 90 days? If not, it can wait.
