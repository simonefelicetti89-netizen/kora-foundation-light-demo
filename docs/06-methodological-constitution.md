# KORA Methodological Constitution

*Version 0.1 — Pre-Calibration*
*Status: Approved — Foundational*

All weights, coefficients and thresholds in this document are provisional. They reflect structured methodological judgment, not statistically validated parameters. Every output produced under this version must carry the notation: **Methodology v0.1 — Pre-Calibration.**

---

## 1. Constitutional Thesis

The central thesis of KORA is that human value inside organizations can be measured — not by counting what is spent, not by cataloguing what is available, not by surveying how people feel — but by structuring, classifying and verifying what people actually did.

KORA transforms real-world human actions into organizational intelligence through a defined methodological chain:

**Real-world action → Structured event → Universal Event Format → Pillar mapping → Quality and verification assessment → Impact Units → Privacy-safe aggregation → Organizational intelligence outputs**

The intelligence outputs of this chain are:
- **KORA Index** — primary measure of the company's human impact program maturity and quality
- **KORA Contribution** — separate measure of verified social and external contribution
- **KORA Ecosystem Reach** — separate measure of partner ecosystem quality and activation
- **Financial Governance indicators** — decision-support intelligence connecting budget, spend and impact
- **Fiscal & Policy Governance indicators** — compliance-support intelligence connecting services, fiscal perimeters and eligibility

KORA does not measure intentions. It does not measure generosity. It does not measure the scale of investment. It measures structured, verified or evidence-supported actions, and derives from them a coherent picture of what human value an organization actually produced.

**The six essential layers KORA always distinguishes:**

| Layer | Definition |
|---|---|
| **INPUT** | Budget, services, programs, partners made available — resources, not results |
| **ACTIVITY** | Events, sessions, completions, participations — something occurred, not yet evidence it mattered |
| **IMPACT** | Verified actions processed into Impact Units, pillar balance, KORA Index — what was produced |
| **GOVERNANCE** | Budget control, cost per outcome, audit trail, decision intelligence — how to act on the intelligence |
| **ELIGIBILITY** | Fiscal/budget perimeter compliance — a governance dimension, not a proxy for impact |
| **EVIDENCE** | Auditability, traceability, versioning — what converts intelligence into credibility |

These six layers must never be collapsed. Every product feature, every dashboard element and every output must be assignable to exactly one of these layers and must not implicitly claim to represent another.

## 2. What Is a KORA Action

A KORA Action is the fundamental unit of KORA methodology. It is a real-world event, participation, completion, contribution or verified behavior that:

1. **Occurred** — it is not a plan, intention, offer, policy, or available resource
2. **Can be attributed** — to an anonymized individual, a cohort, or a defined group
3. **Can be mapped** — to one or more of the five KORA pillars
4. **Carries at least a minimum level of evidence** — whether self-declared, internally recorded, externally verified, or certified

**Examples of valid KORA Actions:**

| Action | Primary Pillar |
|---|---|
| Training course completed | GROWTH |
| Professional certification achieved | GROWTH, LEGACY |
| Health prevention activity attended | LIFE |
| Wellbeing or psychological support session, with privacy-safe metadata only | LIFE |
| Nutrition or physical activity program engagement | LIFE |
| Mentoring session conducted or received | CONNECTION, LEGACY |
| Peer support or knowledge-sharing session | CONNECTION, LEGACY |
| Volunteering activity completed | IMPACT, CONNECTION |
| Social or community project contributed to | IMPACT |
| Environmental or territorial initiative participated in | IMPACT |
| Knowledge transfer session conducted | LEGACY, GROWTH |
| Company-funded people initiative participated in | Pillar depends on initiative type |
| Worker-enabled action through an approved partner | Pillar depends on service |

**What is NOT a KORA Action by itself:**

- Budget allocated to a program, regardless of size
- A benefit or service made available but not used
- A generic ESG or CSR declaration without specific action evidence
- A survey sentiment response without an accompanying verified action
- The number of partners in a catalog
- A marketing claim about company culture or people commitment
- An initiative announcement or policy document
- An unverified declaration with no supporting evidence

The distinction is constitutional: money spent is not the same as human value created. A benefit offered is not the same as a benefit used. A partner available is not the same as a worker activated.

The core unit of KORA is: **verified or evidence-supported action → structured data → analytical processing → impact intelligence.**

Not: money spent → score. Not: benefit offered → score. Not: partners available → score.

## 3. Universal Event Format

KORA receives data from companies operating across multiple disconnected systems. A single company may provide HR records from a HRIS, welfare usage from a welfare platform, training completions from an LMS, ESG declarations from internal spreadsheets, wellbeing provider reports in proprietary formats, and more. Each source uses different terminology, fields, granularity and verification standards.

The Universal Event Format (UEF) is the common language KORA uses to represent all actions from all sources, regardless of origin. It is the bridge between heterogeneous company data and structured KORA intelligence.

The UEF does not flatten actions into identical objects. A high-quality externally certified training program and a self-declared wellness check-in are both processed through the UEF, but they carry different quality, verification and confidence values. The format preserves these differences rather than erasing them.

**Conceptual fields of the Universal Event Format:**

*Identity and attribution — privacy-safe:*
- Anonymized worker identifier or aggregate group reference — never a named individual in employer-visible outputs
- Company identifier
- Department, cohort or job family — only if the group meets safe aggregation thresholds

*Event description:*
- Event type — category of action performed
- Event date or period
- Duration or magnitude, where relevant (hours, sessions, days)
- Continuity or recurrence flag — one-time action or part of ongoing engagement
- Action type — individual, group, or company-wide initiative

*Source and provenance:*
- Source type — HR system, welfare platform, LMS, partner export, internal record, self-declaration
- Source name — specific system or provider that generated the record
- Ingestion confidence — reliability of extraction and processing
- Evidence reference — identifier for supporting documentation where available

*Pillar classification:*
- Primary pillar — main KORA pillar this action contributes to
- Secondary pillars — additional pillar contributions where methodologically justified

*Quality and verification:*
- Content quality — how structured, substantive and meaningful the action is
- Evidence level — how much supporting evidence exists
- Verification level — how trustworthy the source and validation process are
- Impact Confidence — overall reliability of this action as impact evidence

*Privacy and sensitivity:*
- Privacy sensitivity level — low, medium, or high
- Handling protocol reference — which data protection rules apply

*Fiscal and eligibility context:*
- Financial or budget reference, where applicable
- Fiscal/budget perimeter, where applicable
- Eligibility status — Eligible / Conditional / Uncertain / Excluded
- Eligibility confidence — confidence level of the eligibility classification

*Methodology and governance:*
- Methodology version used to process this event
- Review status — reviewed, flagged, or manually approved
- Notes or review flag — free-text for human reviewers

The UEF allows KORA to compare heterogeneous actions on a common analytical scale without pretending all actions are equally valuable, equally reliable, or equally well-evidenced. Every event enters KORA through the UEF and is never processed in its raw source format.

## 4. The Five-Pillar Model

The five pillars are the grammar of KORA. They are the classification system that translates heterogeneous human actions into a common, comparable organizational language. They are not decorative categories. They are not user interface labels. Every analytical output of KORA is expressed in terms of these pillars.

---

**LIFE**
Health, wellbeing, prevention, psychological support, nutrition, physical activity, safety-related wellbeing and quality of life. Actions that help people live and work better — physically, mentally and emotionally. LIFE represents the foundation of sustainable human performance. Without it, all other pillars are fragile.

**GROWTH**
Learning, professional development, skills acquisition, certifications, digital upskilling, personal evolution and human capital development. Not merely access to courses, but demonstrated engagement with learning that builds usable knowledge and skill.

**CONNECTION**
Mentoring, peer support, collaboration, internal communities, cross-functional engagement, team cohesion and the relational fabric of the organization. Relational quality is not a soft metric — it is a structural determinant of organizational resilience and innovation capacity.

**IMPACT**
Volunteering, social projects, community support, environmental initiatives, school orientation, territorial contribution and verified value generated beyond the individual and beyond the company. Not declared intent, but verified participation in external value creation.

**LEGACY**
Knowledge transfer, senior-junior mentorship, organizational memory, durable practices, cultural continuity and value that remains after a single action is completed. The long time-horizon of human capital — the wisdom, practice and institutional knowledge that outlast any individual role.

---

**Pillar mapping rules:**

Each KORA Action carries one primary pillar. Where the nature of the action justifiably contributes to more than one pillar, secondary assignments are permitted — but must be methodologically justified, not used to inflate multi-pillar scoring.

| Action | Primary | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Gym / health prevention activity | LIFE | — |
| Certification or advanced training | GROWTH | LEGACY |
| Mentoring relationship | CONNECTION | LEGACY |
| Volunteering project | IMPACT | CONNECTION |
| Knowledge transfer session | LEGACY | GROWTH |
| Peer learning community | CONNECTION | GROWTH |
| Company-wide social project | IMPACT | CONNECTION |
| Occupational health prevention | LIFE | — |

**Why pillar balance matters:**

KORA must not reward volume. It must reward balance, quality and breadth. A company concentrating 80% of its people activity in GROWTH while neglecting LIFE, CONNECTION, IMPACT and LEGACY is not producing well-rounded human value — it is optimizing for one dimension at the expense of others. The shape of human value generated by the organization is more important than the total quantity of actions counted.

## 5. Impact Units

Impact Units (IU) are the normalized analytical output generated by KORA Actions after classification, quality assessment, verification, continuity adjustment and anti-gaming processing. They are the internal currency of KORA's analytical engine.

**Impact Units are:**
- An internal analytical unit, not displayed as a raw number in most employer-facing contexts
- A bridge between individual raw events and company-level intelligence
- A method for quantifying relative contribution across pillars with sensitivity to quality and verification
- The foundation for calculating the KORA Index, KORA Contribution, KORA Ecosystem Reach and financial governance ratios

**Impact Units are NOT:**
- Money — they cannot be redeemed, exchanged or transferred
- Employee rewards or gamification points
- Individual performance scores or merit measures
- Moral judgments about the value of a person
- Absolute scientific measurements — they are an analytical instrument subject to calibration and revision

**Prototype Impact Unit formula:**

```
IU_{e,p} = NM_e × BC_{e,p} × CQ_e × EV_e × CF_e × AGF_e × DF_e × EXF_e
```

Where:

| Symbol | Name | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| IU_{e,p} | Impact Units | Units generated by event *e* on pillar *p* |
| NM_e | Normalized Magnitude | Normalized measure of action size (duration, sessions, depth), capped to prevent manipulation |
| BC_{e,p} | Base Contribution | Provisional base weight of this event type to pillar *p* |
| CQ_e | Content Quality | Multiplier reflecting how structured, substantive and meaningful the action is |
| EV_e | Evidence / Verification Level | Multiplier reflecting how reliably the action has been verified |
| CF_e | Continuity Factor | Multiplier for whether the action is part of a sustained engagement vs a one-time event |
| AGF_e | Anti-Gaming Factor | Reduction applied when patterns suggest repetition or inflation |
| DF_e | Durability Factor | Optional — additional weight for LEGACY actions with demonstrably long-lasting value |
| EXF_e | Externality Factor | Optional — additional weight for IMPACT actions with verified external/territorial value |

**Notes on DF and EXF:**
- DF applies primarily to LEGACY pillar actions where the knowledge or practice transferred has documented long-term organizational effect
- EXF applies primarily to IMPACT pillar actions where external community or territorial value has been verified by a qualified partner or third party
- When not applicable, both default to **1.00** — they do not reduce IU, they are simply neutral
- Both are provisional and require calibration from real pilot data before being activated

**All coefficients, weights and factors are provisional.** They reflect structured judgment in v0.1 and must be calibrated through real company pilots, expert review and statistical validation before any coefficient is treated as methodologically definitive.

## 6. Quality, Verification & Impact Confidence

Not all KORA Actions carry equal methodological weight. A company submitting externally certified, partner-verified records is in a fundamentally different evidential position than one self-declaring all activity. KORA must recognize this distinction explicitly and reflect it in every output.

**Source Type**
The origin of the action record. Source type defines the starting context for all quality and verification assessment.

| Source Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Self-declaration | Worker or company asserts the action occurred; no independent verification |
| Internal company record | HR system, initiative log, internal database — recorded but not externally validated |
| External provider record | Export from a welfare or training provider; no KORA certification |
| KORA Partner record | Export from a KORA network partner with documented eligibility profile |
| KORA Certified Partner record | Highest partner verification; partner has passed KORA certification process |
| External certification record | Examination result, professional accreditation, recognized third-party award |
| Future: KORA Link event | Real-time physical or digital verification at the point of action |

**Content Quality (CQ)**
How structured, substantive and meaningful is the action itself?
- A three-day structured training program with assessment → higher CQ
- A thirty-minute awareness webinar → lower CQ
- A multi-session mentoring relationship with defined goals → higher CQ
- A single informal conversation logged as mentoring → lower CQ
Content Quality distinguishes depth from surface — genuine engagement from checkbox participation.

**Evidence Level (EV component)**
How much supporting evidence exists for this action?

| Level | Description | EV multiplier (provisional) |
|---|---|---|
| No evidence | Declared; nothing supports it | 0.40 |
| Minimal evidence | Timestamp or enrollment record only | 0.55 |
| Moderate evidence | Completion record from internal system | 0.70 |
| Strong evidence | Completion record plus documentation | 0.85 |
| Certified evidence | Independently verified by qualified external source | 1.00 |

**Verification Level**
The combined trustworthiness of source type and evidence level. Verification Level is the input to the EV factor in the Impact Unit formula.

**Impact Confidence**
The summary reliability measure for each event and for aggregated outputs. Impact Confidence answers: how much methodological trust should be placed in this action's Impact Units as evidence of real human impact?

Impact Confidence is not binary. It is a spectrum. High-confidence certified events carry full analytical weight. Low-confidence self-declared events carry partial weight. The difference must be visible in every output.

**Auditability**
Whether the event record and its calculation can be reconstructed later, step by step, using the methodology version in effect at the time. Auditability is a binary property: an event either has a complete, traceable record or it does not.

**If data quality is low, KORA shows it. KORA does not fabricate precision from weak inputs.**

The principle: a low-quality dataset produces lower Impact Confidence, not a false appearance of certainty. An honest output with visible confidence levels is more valuable than a precise-looking output that conceals its assumptions. Trust is built through transparency about limitation, not through the illusion of completeness.

## 7. Privacy-by-Design & Trust Layer

KORA must be privacy-by-design from the first version. Privacy cannot be retrofitted later without architectural cost and reputational risk.

If KORA is perceived as a tool through which employers surveil, evaluate or rank their employees, the product fails — commercially, ethically and legally. KORA must be designed to make this outcome structurally impossible, not merely policy-prohibited.

**Aggregation before exposure**
The employer sees workforce intelligence. The employer does not see individual surveillance. This is not a feature toggle — it is an architectural principle that must be enforced at the data layer, not only at the interface layer.

**Pseudonymization and anonymization by default**
Worker-level data used for internal calculation must be pseudonymized at ingestion. Identifiers are separated from analytical records before any processing occurs. KORA should never require knowing who specifically did something in order to tell the company what its workforce collectively produced.

**No employer-visible individual scoring**
The employer must not see named, identified or individually attributable PIB scores, impact ratings, welfare usage records, or health-related participation data. The employer sees distributions, trends, bands and aggregates — never the individual behind them.

**Minimum necessary data**
KORA should ingest only what is necessary to calculate impact intelligence. If a field does not contribute to KORA's analytical outputs, it should not be collected. If a more aggregated version of the data is sufficient, the more granular version must not be required.

**Sensitive data protection**
Health records, psychological support attendance, wellbeing session participation, disability accommodations, family support services and any data that could reveal personal medical, psychological or social conditions require heightened protection beyond standard anonymization.

What the employer may see regarding psychological support:
- Aggregate session count in the workforce during the period
- LIFE pillar contribution associated with this service category
- Anonymized participation band

What the employer must never see:
- Which employees attended
- Diagnosis, reason for support, or session content
- Therapist or provider notes
- Any individually identifiable pattern

**Safe aggregation thresholds**
Segment-level analytics must only be shown when the group is large enough that individual identification is not reasonably possible. Default minimum: **10 individuals per segment**. This threshold is configurable by legal counsel based on jurisdiction and data category.

**No punitive use**
KORA must not be designed, configured or used to evaluate, rank, reward or penalize individual employees. This principle must be embedded in KORA's terms of service, data processing agreements and product design.

**Trust is a product feature**
Privacy is not a compliance tax. It is a central part of KORA's commercial value proposition. A product that companies trust with sensitive workforce data is a structurally stronger commercial position than one that merely avoids legal violations. Companies, workers, regulators and advisors must all be able to trust KORA's outputs precisely because they know what KORA does not do with individual data.

## 8. GDPR / Legal Privacy Governance

*Conceptual alignment only. Not legal advice. Legal counsel must review and validate KORA's data processing architecture, agreements and procedures before commercial deployment.*

KORA's design must be consistent with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and applicable national data protection frameworks.

**Data minimization:** Only data necessary for KORA's stated analytical purposes should be collected and processed.

**Purpose limitation:** Data collected for KORA's impact intelligence function must not be repurposed without explicit new legal basis.

**Pseudonymization and anonymization:** Worker-level records used in analytical processing must be pseudonymized at ingestion. Outputs designed to be truly anonymous — where re-identification is not reasonably possible — may carry a different legal treatment and should be engineered to meet that standard where possible.

**Legal basis:** The basis for processing workforce data (likely legitimate interest, contractual necessity, or explicit consent depending on data category and use) must be determined and documented with legal counsel before deployment. Special categories of personal data (health, psychological support, disability) require explicit legal basis — typically explicit consent or specific legal permission under applicable national law.

**Data Processing Agreement (DPA):** Companies using KORA are data controllers. KORA is a data processor. A DPA must be in place before any company data is processed, specifying purposes, data categories, retention periods, sub-processors and security measures.

**Retention policy:** Data must not be retained for longer than necessary for KORA's analytical purposes. A defined retention schedule must be established per data category.

**Deletion and export rights:** KORA must support the right of data subjects to request deletion or export of their personal data, within the constraints of applicable anonymization rules.

**Access control:** Role-based access control must restrict each internal role and company stakeholder to only the data their function requires.

**Audit logs:** All access to company data and all significant processing operations must be logged.

**DPIA:** A Data Protection Impact Assessment must be conducted before processing sensitive data categories at scale. Health, psychological wellbeing and social vulnerability categories require this assessment before deployment.

**No automated individual decision-making visible to employer:** KORA must not produce employer-visible outputs that constitute automated individual decision-making with significant effects on employees — regulated under GDPR Article 22.

**Architectural separation:** The separation between employer-visible aggregate intelligence and protected individual data must be enforced at the system level, not only at the interface level. Interface-layer restrictions that can be bypassed by API calls or database access do not satisfy this requirement.

## 9. Data Sensitivity Classification

Every event type processed by KORA carries a sensitivity classification. This classification governs privacy handling protocols, aggregation thresholds and field-level retention decisions.

**Low Sensitivity**
- Generic training or course participation (non-sensitive topic areas)
- Public volunteering event attendance
- Generic company initiative or cultural program participation
- Professional certification achievement
- Non-sensitive course completion records

*Handling:* Standard anonymization protocols. Employer-visible aggregation permitted above safe group-size thresholds.

**Medium Sensitivity**
- Department-level or cohort-level participation data
- Mentoring participation (role and frequency, not content)
- Welfare service usage by category (without individual identification)
- Internal project involvement
- Job family or seniority cohort analytics
- Cross-functional participation patterns

*Handling:* Pseudonymization required at worker level. Employer visibility at segment level only, above safe thresholds. Individual level suppressed.

**High Sensitivity**
- Health prevention activities (medical check-ups, screening programs)
- Psychological support service attendance
- Mental health or wellbeing path engagement
- Disability support, social vulnerability or family crisis service usage
- Any data that could reveal personal medical, psychological, financial or family conditions
- Special categories of personal data under GDPR Article 9

*Handling:* Highest privacy protection. Employer visibility limited to aggregate counts and anonymized trends. No segment-level display below safe thresholds. No individual identifiability. Small group suppression enforced. Only privacy-safe metadata processed in KORA analytical layers. Raw data is never exposed to the employer.

## 10. Employer Visibility Matrix

This matrix defines what companies can and cannot see within KORA. It is a constitutional boundary — not a feature preference — and must be enforced at the architectural level.

**The employer MAY see:**

*Aggregate workforce intelligence:*
- Total activation rate (% of workforce with at least one verified action in the period)
- Meaningful activation rate (% with quality-weighted sustained engagement)
- Anonymized participation distribution bands

*Pillar-level outputs:*
- KORA Index, KORA Contribution, KORA Ecosystem Reach
- Pillar balance and distribution across the five pillars
- Pillar strength, weakness and trend over time

*Segment analytics — above safe thresholds only:*
- Activation by department or cohort where group size is above threshold
- Job family or seniority band trends where privacy-safe

*Quality and confidence signals:*
- Impact Confidence score
- Data quality indicators and completeness warnings
- Verification rate (% of actions with supporting evidence)
- Confidence score (overall methodological reliability of the KORA Index)

*Financial governance:*
- Budget allocated vs used by pillar and initiative
- Cost per Impact Unit by pillar and provider
- Partner utilization and efficiency signals
- Underfunded and overfunded pillar alerts

*Fiscal and eligibility context:*
- Budget category distribution
- Eligibility confidence distribution
- Policy rule compliance status
- Audit trail summaries

*Reporting outputs:*
- KORA Evolution timeline
- Aggregate risk and opportunity signals
- Recommended rebalancing directions

**The employer MUST NOT see:**

- Named or individually identifiable PIB scores
- Named or identifiable individual action records
- Any record linking a specific named person to a sensitive service
- Diagnosis or reason for any health-related service use
- Psychological support session content, frequency or provider at individual level
- Medical condition, treatment or screening results
- Disability status, accommodation details or vulnerability indicators
- Therapist, counsellor or social support professional notes
- Family crisis support usage at individual level
- Segment data where the group falls below the safe aggregation threshold, regardless of sensitivity
- Individual-level rankings, comparative scores or performance indicators designed to evaluate employees
- Any individual surveillance output of any kind

This matrix is a core trust mechanism for KORA. Any feature request or configuration that would require breaching this matrix must be refused, regardless of the company's stated preference.

## 11. PIB — Personal Impact Balance

PIB is the individual-level analytical balance of Impact Units accumulated by a worker across the five KORA pillars over a defined period.

**Prototype PIB formula:**

```
PIB_worker = Σ_p IU_{worker,p}
```

Where the sum is across all five pillars *p*, aggregating all Impact Units generated by that worker's verified actions over the reference period.

PIB may be decomposed by pillar: PIB_LIFE, PIB_GROWTH, PIB_CONNECTION, PIB_IMPACT, PIB_LEGACY — giving a five-dimensional profile of the worker's impact balance.

**PIB is a computational instrument, not a product for employer consumption.**

KORA uses PIB internally to calculate:
- Activation rate (is this worker active above the threshold?)
- Meaningful activation (does this worker's PIB meet quality criteria?)
- Distribution (how evenly is impact distributed across the workforce?)
- Recurrence (does this worker engage consistently over time?)
- Concentration risk (is organizational impact driven by 5% of workers?)
- Pillar balance at workforce level

**PIB is not:**
- Employee performance evaluation
- Productivity or efficiency score
- Attendance or compliance record
- Merit ranking
- Disciplinary evidence
- HR management tool of any kind

**In Foundation Light:** PIB exists only as a pseudonymized internal analytical layer. The employer sees only aggregated distributions derived from PIB — activation rates, participation bands, pillar distribution — never the PIB value of any identified individual.

**In future worker-facing modules:** A worker-facing PIB view may become a product module — a personal dashboard the worker controls to understand their own impact balance. This module, if built, must be controlled by the worker, not the employer; designed with informed consent and full transparency; and never accessible to the employer in individual form.

PIB belongs to the worker's understanding of their own development, not to the employer's evaluation of their contribution.

## 12. From Individual Layer to Company Intelligence

The analytical path from individual records to company intelligence is a one-way bridge. Data flows from worker-level records into aggregated intelligence. It does not flow back into employer-visible individual identification.

KORA needs pseudonymized worker-level records internally because:
- Activation Rate requires counting distinct active individuals
- Meaningful Activation requires assessing individual PIB quality
- Concentration risk requires understanding how impact is distributed
- Participation equity requires tracking patterns across workforce segments
- Continuity requires tracking whether the same worker engages across multiple periods

These requirements make pseudonymized worker-level records computationally necessary. They do not make employer-visible individual records acceptable.

**The architectural commitment:** Worker-level records are processed inside the analytical engine. What emerges on the employer-facing side is workforce intelligence — distributions, bands, rates, trends — never individual surveillance.

The rule: **workforce intelligence, not employee surveillance.**

KORA's value is not in telling companies which employees are "doing well" by an arbitrary metric. Its value is in telling companies what human value their organization is collectively producing, where the gaps are, and what should change.

## 13. Company Total Impact Units

The aggregate sum of all Impact Units generated across the workforce is a useful volume metric.

**Formula:**

```
Company Total IU = Σ_worker PIB_worker
```

Company Total IU provides a sense of the overall scale of verified human activity in the organization. It can be decomposed by pillar:

```
Company IU_LIFE = Σ_worker PIB_{worker, LIFE}
```

And similarly for GROWTH, CONNECTION, IMPACT and LEGACY.

**Critical clarification:** Company Total IU is not the KORA Index. A company with high Total IU may still score poorly on the KORA Index if that volume is concentrated in one pillar, driven by a small group of workers, dominated by low-quality self-declared events, or shows no continuity over time. Volume without quality, balance and distribution is not organizational impact maturity.

## 14. KORA Index

The KORA Index is the primary company-level score produced by KORA. It measures the maturity and quality of the company's human impact program — not its size, not its spending, not the ambitions of its leadership.

**KORA Index is NOT:**
- A simple total of Impact Units
- A weighted average of PIB across the workforce
- A measure of how much budget was spent
- A measure of how many partners are available
- A measure of how many services are offered to employees
- A measure of fiscal eligibility or tax efficiency
- A score that can be inflated through volume without quality
- A replacement for or equivalent to ESG disclosure
- An evaluation of individual employee performance

**Prototype KORA Index formula:**

```
KORA Index = 100 × Σ_k (Score_k × Weight_k)
```

Where *k* iterates over the component scores below.

**Prototype component scores and provisional weights:**

| Component | Formula reference | Provisional weight |
|---|---|---|
| Activation Rate | Section 15 | 20% |
| Normalized Intensity | Section 16 | 20% |
| Pillar Balance | Section 17 | 15% |
| Event Quality | Section 18 | 15% |
| Verification Rate | Section 19 | 10% |
| Continuity | Section 20 | 10% |
| Pillar Coverage | Section 21 | 10% |
| **Total** | | **100%** |

Equity/Fairness signals (Section 29) and Confidence adjustment are currently dashboard indicators, not KORA Index components. If either becomes a formal component in a future methodology version, the weights above must be recalibrated and the version number incremented.

**What the KORA Index must resist:**
- High activity in one pillar must not produce a high overall Index score
- High budget spend must not produce a high score if activation is low
- A large partner catalog must not produce a high score if workers are not using services
- Self-declared activity at scale must not produce the same score as verified activity at the same scale
- Impact concentrated in 10% of workers must not produce the same score as impact distributed across 70%
- An unexplained spike in reported events should trigger confidence review, not a score increase

Every KORA Index score must be explainable. A company must be able to understand which components are strong, which are weak, and what the principal data gaps are. Every score carries a methodology version reference.

## 15. Activation Rate

Activation Rate measures what proportion of the eligible workforce has engaged in at least one verified KORA Action during the reference period at or above a minimum quality threshold.

**Formula:**

```
Activation Rate = Active Workers / Total Eligible Workers
```

Where:

- **Active Workers** = workers whose PIB in the reference period meets or exceeds the provisional activation threshold
- **Total Eligible Workers** = total headcount eligible for the people program in the reference period (may exclude workers on extended leave or not yet enrolled, depending on company policy)

**Prototype activation threshold:** PIB ≥ minimum threshold T_activate (provisional value: to be calibrated per pilot; indicative starting range 5–15 IU per reference period).

The threshold must be low enough to count genuine but modest engagement, and high enough to exclude noise (one trivial self-declared action per year that a company would not claim as program participation).

This threshold is provisional and must be calibrated from real pilot data.

## 16. Normalized Intensity

Normalized Intensity measures the depth and richness of engagement among active workers — going beyond the binary question of whether someone activated to ask how meaningfully they engaged.

**Formula:**

```
Normalized Intensity = Average PIB of Active Workers / Reference PIB Benchmark
```

Where:

- **Average PIB of Active Workers** = Company Total IU / Active Workers
- **Reference PIB Benchmark** = a provisional reference value representing a meaningful level of sustained annual engagement (to be calibrated; indicative initial value: 100 IU per year for a full-year program)

A Normalized Intensity of 1.00 means active workers are engaging at the reference benchmark level on average. Values above 1.00 indicate richer-than-benchmark engagement; values below 1.00 indicate thinner engagement among those who are active.

The benchmark is provisional and will eventually be normalized by sector, company size, workforce structure and program maturity as the KORA network grows.

---

## 17. Pillar Balance

Pillar Balance measures how evenly the company's verified impact is distributed across the five KORA pillars. A program concentrated in one or two pillars, regardless of how much total impact it produces in those pillars, represents a structural imbalance.

**Conceptual formula:**

```
Pillar Balance = 1 − (normalized average absolute deviation from target pillar distribution)
```

Where:

- The **target pillar distribution** in v0.1 is equal distribution: 20% per pillar
- The **deviation** for each pillar is |actual share − target share|
- The **normalized average absolute deviation** scales the raw deviation to a 0–1 range so that maximum imbalance (all impact in one pillar) yields Pillar Balance = 0 and perfect balance yields Pillar Balance = 1

A Pillar Balance of 1.00 means the company's impact is perfectly distributed across all five pillars. A score of 0.60 means meaningful concentration in one or two pillars with others receiving significantly less.

The equal distribution target in v0.1 is provisional. In future methodology versions, the target may become sector-specific (e.g., a healthcare company may legitimately weight LIFE more heavily) or strategy-specific (e.g., a company in a digital transformation phase may legitimately weight GROWTH). Any change to the target distribution requires a methodology version increment.

---

## 18. Event Quality

Event Quality measures the average quality of the verified actions that generated Impact Units in the reference period, weighted by their contribution.

**Formula:**

```
Event Quality = Σ_e (IU_e × CQ_e × EV_e) / Σ_e IU_e
```

A portfolio of high-quality, sustained, externally verified actions produces a higher Event Quality score than a larger portfolio of low-quality, self-declared, one-time events.

Event Quality penalizes the strategy of substituting quantity for quality — accumulating many thin, unverified events to generate high Total IU while the actual depth of human engagement is low.

---

## 19. Verification Rate

Verification Rate measures the proportion of the company's Impact Units that are supported by evidence at or above a defined threshold — distinguishing companies with strong data provenance from those relying predominantly on self-declaration.

**Formula:**

```
Verification Rate = IU from evidenced or verified actions / Total IU
```

Where "evidenced or verified" means EV ≥ moderate evidence (EV multiplier ≥ 0.70 in the provisional scale defined in Section 6).

A company where 80% of Total IU comes from self-declared actions with no supporting evidence carries a lower Verification Rate — and a lower KORA Index component score for this dimension — than one where 80% comes from externally verified or certified events.

---

## 20. Continuity

Continuity measures whether the company's workforce is engaging in sustained, recurring patterns of human development rather than isolated, episodic activity.

**Formula:**

```
Continuity Score = (Workers with recurring or multi-session engagement + Actions within structured programs) / All Active Workers and Actions
```

This is expressed as a weighted share of recurrent engagement within the total activated population and activity portfolio.

Continuity rewards programs that build sustained habits and structured development paths rather than one-off events. A single annual team-building day does not generate the same Continuity Score as a quarterly learning program, a multi-session mentoring relationship, or a year-long wellbeing path.

**Important constraint:** Repeated low-value events must be capped by the Anti-Gaming Factor (Section 32) before Continuity is calculated. Artificially inflating Continuity by logging many trivial recurring actions must not increase the score.

---

## 21. Pillar Coverage

Pillar Coverage measures how many of the five pillars receive meaningful verified activation — distinguishing companies with broad, inclusive programs from those with strong depth in one or two areas and nothing in others.

**Formula:**

```
Pillar Coverage = Pillars above minimum meaningful threshold / 5
```

Where the **minimum meaningful threshold** for a pillar is provisional: a pillar is considered meaningfully covered when its share of Total IU exceeds a minimum threshold T_pillar (indicative starting value: 5% of Total IU, meaning each pillar receives at least some genuine verified activity).

A company with meaningful activation in all five pillars scores 1.00. A company with meaningful activation in only three pillars scores 0.60. A company with meaningful activation in only one pillar scores 0.20.

The threshold T_pillar is provisional and must be calibrated from real pilot data to ensure it distinguishes genuine coverage from token activity.

---

## 22. KORA Contribution

KORA Contribution is a separate indicator measuring the social, territorial and external contribution generated by the organization — distinct from the internal human development maturity measured by the KORA Index.

KORA Contribution is related primarily to the IMPACT pillar but is not simply the IMPACT pillar score. It measures a specific dimension of value: the organization's verified contribution to the world beyond its own workforce and operations.

**Prototype formula:**

```
KORA Contribution = w1 × Worker Coverage (IMPACT) + w2 × Impact Intensity + w3 × Externality Verification + w4 × Partner Quality + w5 × Territorial Coverage + w6 × Contribution Continuity
```

All weights w1–w6 are provisional and sum to 1.00. They must be calibrated from real data.

**Why KORA Contribution must remain separate from the KORA Index:**

A company may have strong internal people-impact maturity — high activation, good pillar balance, quality learning and wellbeing programs — while generating minimal verified external contribution. The KORA Index should reflect internal strength accurately without crediting external contribution it did not produce.

Conversely, a company may have a small group generating exceptional external social impact while the broader workforce has low overall activation. KORA Contribution should reflect that external strength without masking the internal weakness.

Merging the two into a single indicator makes both less interpretable and less actionable.

---

## 23. KORA Ecosystem Reach

KORA Ecosystem Reach measures the quality, coverage and actual utilization of the partner and service ecosystem available to the company's workforce.

**Prototype formula:**

```
KORA Ecosystem Reach = w1 × Partner Utilization + w2 × Certification Ratio + w3 × Territorial Reach + w4 × Service Diversity + w5 × Pillar Coverage of Partner Network + w6 × Concentration Balance
```

All weights w1–w6 are provisional and sum to 1.00.

**Why partner availability alone is not impact:**

A company may maintain a large, diverse catalog of partner services that workers never use. The catalog represents potential, not realized value. KORA Ecosystem Reach must distinguish between the richness of what is available and the activation of what is actually used.

Partner availability can influence the KORA Index indirectly — and only — when it leads to verified worker actions that generate Impact Units. The existence of partners in a catalog does not improve the KORA Index directly.

KORA Ecosystem Reach is therefore a diagnostic tool: it tells the company whether its ecosystem is rich enough, balanced enough and activated enough to support the impact program it is trying to build.

---

## 24. Financial Governance Relationship

Financial governance is a decision-support layer that runs alongside impact intelligence. It does not determine the KORA Index. It helps companies understand the relationship between what they invest and what they receive in return.

**Core financial governance formulas:**

```
Cost per Impact Unit = Total relevant spend / Company Total IU

Cost per Pillar IU = Spend allocated to pillar p / IU generated in pillar p

Budget Activation Ratio = Budget used / Budget allocated

Spend-to-Impact Efficiency = Company Total IU / Total relevant spend (IU per euro)
```

**The governance narrative these formulas support:**
- Which pillars generate the most verified impact per euro spent?
- Which partners are efficient in generating verified activation?
- Which areas are underfunded relative to their impact potential?
- Which areas absorb spending without generating verified activation?
- Where should the next budget allocation go?

**The line that must never be crossed:**

Financial data must not corrupt the impact score. A company with a large welfare budget and low activation must not score well on the KORA Index. A company with a modest budget and strong, well-distributed, verified activation must not score poorly. Budget is a resource. What matters is what the resource produced.

Financial Governance is a decision layer. Impact Intelligence is the value layer. They serve the same organizational goal — better human capital decisions — through different analytical lenses.

---

## 25. Fiscal & Policy Eligibility Layer

The Fiscal & Policy Eligibility Layer is a constitutional component of KORA methodology, not an add-on. It addresses a dimension of organizational reality that impact intelligence alone cannot cover: the fiscal, budgetary and regulatory framework through which companies fund and govern their people programs.

**The four dimensions KORA maintains separately for every action and service:**

1. **Impact Pillar** — What human value does this action generate? (LIFE / GROWTH / CONNECTION / IMPACT / LEGACY)

2. **Fiscal/Budget Perimeter** — Through which company budget or fiscal framework can this action be funded?
Examples: welfare budget / fringe benefit / training budget / health & wellbeing budget / people or HR budget / ESG or CSR budget / non-tax-advantaged corporate budget / custom internal policy budget

3. **Partner/Service Eligibility** — Is this specific partner or service compatible with the selected perimeter?
Status: Eligible / Conditional / Uncertain / Excluded

4. **Policy Rules** — What company-specific constraints apply?
Caps, approved providers, eligible worker categories, documentation requirements, approval workflows, reporting obligations

**The principle that must never be violated:**

Fiscal eligibility is not impact. Impact is not fiscal eligibility. A service can be fiscally eligible and generate low human impact. A service can generate exceptional human impact and be fiscally ineligible under the specific perimeter a company wants to use. KORA surfaces both dimensions clearly and separately in all outputs.

**KORA's scope:**

KORA provides the operating layer that maps services, partners and actions against the fiscal/budget rules that the company and its advisors define. KORA does not provide legal advice, tax advice or binding fiscal eligibility determinations. The legal and fiscal validity of eligibility classifications is the company's responsibility, validated with qualified labor law, tax and welfare specialists.

---

## 26. Eligibility Status & Eligibility Confidence

Every fiscal/service eligibility classification in KORA carries two parallel attributes that must never be conflated.

**Eligibility Status — what the classification is:**

| Status | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Eligible** | Compatible with this fiscal/budget perimeter; standard documentation applies |
| **Conditional** | Potentially compatible, but only if specific conditions are met; conditions must be surfaced |
| **Uncertain** | Compatibility is not clearly established; advisor consultation required before tax-advantaged activation |
| **Excluded** | Not compatible with this specific perimeter; may be available under another perimeter |

**Eligibility Confidence — what supports the classification:**

| Confidence Level | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Advisor-Confirmed** | Reviewed and confirmed by a qualified labor law advisor, tax consultant or welfare specialist |
| **KORA Advisor-Confirmed** | Reviewed and confirmed by a KORA-authorized advisor through the KORA advisor network |
| **Partner-Documented** | Supported by formal compliance documentation provided by the partner; not independently reviewed |
| **Partner-Declared** | Asserted by the partner without supporting documentation; KORA has not verified the basis |
| **KORA-Inferred** | Derived by KORA from taxonomy and rule-based classification; starting point only, not audit-grade |
| **Pending Review** | Submitted for review; not yet resolved; should not be activated under tax-advantaged perimeters |
| **Outdated — Requires Review** | Previously confirmed but rendered potentially stale by a triggering event |

**How Eligibility Confidence differs from Impact Confidence:**

*Impact Confidence* answers: how reliable is this action as evidence of human impact? Determined by source quality, evidence level and verification. Affects the KORA Index.

*Eligibility Confidence* answers: how reliable is the judgment that this service is fiscally eligible under this perimeter? Determined by who validated the classification and whether it is current. Does not affect the KORA Index.

These operate on different dimensions and must never be merged. A service can have high Impact Confidence and low Eligibility Confidence — generating excellent impact data while carrying unresolved fiscal compliance risk. The company needs to see both signals clearly.

**Versioning of eligibility classifications:**

Every eligibility record carries: the status at time of classification, the confidence level, the source of the validation, the date, the fiscal/regulatory context applied, and the expiry condition or review trigger. When a classification is updated, the prior version is retained for audit. KORA proactively flags Outdated classifications when triggering events occur — new fiscal legislation, service restructuring, policy changes, or review cycle expiry.

## 27. Partner & Service Eligibility Mapping

Every partner service in the KORA network must carry a complete, current eligibility profile. Eligibility profiling is a condition of network participation.

**Required eligibility profile fields per service:**

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Impact pillar(s) | Which KORA pillars does this service contribute to? |
| Fiscal/budget eligibility | Eligibility status per perimeter (Eligible / Conditional / Uncertain / Excluded) |
| Eligibility confidence | Confidence level per perimeter |
| Eligibility conditions | What conditions must be met (for Conditional status) |
| Required documentation | What evidence must be on record for eligible or conditional activations |
| Verification level | What level of Impact Confidence KORA assigns to confirmed usage |
| Territory/country | In which countries is this service available and the fiscal eligibility valid? |
| Privacy sensitivity | Low / Medium / High — which data protection protocols apply |
| Action type | One-time event, recurring enrollment, or structured program |
| Last review date | Date of the most recent eligibility profile review |
| Review trigger | What events require a new review (annual renewal, legislation change, etc.) |

When a company configures its fiscal/budget perimeters, KORA uses these profiles to filter the catalog, surface eligibility status and confidence, and warn on conditional, uncertain or outdated classifications. This is what makes KORA operationally useful, not only analytically useful: a company does not just learn about its past impact — it can govern its future program selections with fiscal intelligence embedded in the process.

## 28. Policy Rules Engine

The policy rules engine is the company-specific configuration layer within KORA's governance architecture. It translates the company's internal policy decisions into enforced operational rules within the platform.

**Policy rules may include:**

| Rule type | Description |
|---|---|
| Spending caps | Maximum per employee, per period, per perimeter, or per service category |
| Eligible worker categories | Restrictions by job family, seniority, contract type, location or tenure |

**Scope of KORA's role:**

The company and its advisors define the policy rules. KORA configures, enforces, records and audits them. It provides the infrastructure to implement and track the decisions made by those qualified to make them.

## 29. Fairness & Equity Layer

KORA does not only measure how much impact the organization produces in aggregate. It must also measure how impact is distributed. A program generating excellent aggregate KORA Index scores driven primarily by 10% of the workforce — typically the most senior, most privileged or most engaged employees — is not a well-governed human impact program.

**Equity signals KORA tracks:**

| Signal | Definition |
|---|---|
| Bottom 50% Activation | What share of the lower 50% of workers (by PIB) have any activation at all? |
| Top 10% Concentration | What percentage of Company Total IU is generated by the top 10% of PIB holders? |
| Department Activation Gap | Difference in activation rate between the highest and lowest activated departments |
| Pillar Access Gap | Are specific worker segments systematically excluded from access to certain pillar categories? |
| PIB Gini Coefficient | An exploratory measure of inequality in PIB distribution across the workforce |

The Gini coefficient is provided as an exploratory metric — it is familiar in economics and can be calculated from PIB distributions. It is not yet a KORA Index component.

**When equity signals become alarming:**
- Top 10% concentration above 50% of Total IU signals that the program is serving a small group rather than the workforce
- Bottom 50% activation below 30% signals structural exclusion from program participation
- Department activation gaps above 40 percentage points signal that some parts of the organization are not reached at all

Equity signals are currently dashboard indicators. Whether they become formal KORA Index components depends on validation and expert review. The methodological principle: **a strong KORA program is broad, balanced, verified and inclusive.**

## 30. Benchmark & Normalization Layer

KORA Index scores must eventually be normalized to be meaningful across different companies, sectors and contexts. A manufacturing company with a largely operational workforce is not directly comparable to a professional services firm with a highly mobile, highly educated workforce. Comparing them without normalization produces misleading conclusions.

**Normalization dimensions KORA must eventually account for:**
- Company size (headcount bands)
- Sector or industry classification
- Geography and applicable regulatory environment
- Workforce structure (office, remote, operational, frontline composition)
- Data maturity (how long has the company been using KORA? How complete is its data?)
- Program maturity (first year of a people program vs multi-year established program)
- Regulatory obligations that drive baseline activity in specific sectors
- Type of work (shift-based vs project-based vs continuous)

**Phase-appropriate approach:**

*Foundation Light:* Internal provisional normalization only. No cross-company comparison. The KORA Index is meaningful relative to the company's own data and the methodology's internal standards.

*Foundation:* Longitudinal internal comparison. The company tracks its own improvement relative to its own baseline.

*Governance:* Sector and size benchmarks may be introduced as the KORA network grows, with appropriate anonymization.

*Certified:* Benchmark methodology must be strengthened and externally validated before it is used to support certification claims.

**The warning against unfair comparison:**

A company in a heavily regulated sector with mandatory safety and training programs may appear highly activated not because its program is excellent but because compliance obligations drive baseline activity. A company in a lightly regulated sector with voluntary, high-quality programs may appear less activated despite superior program design. Normalization must account for these structural differences. Raw KORA Index comparisons across sectors without normalization must never be used in external communications.

## 31. Outcome Correlation Layer

Future versions of KORA may connect impact indicators to measurable organizational outcomes — building the evidence base for KORA's core commercial proposition: that well-structured human impact investment generates return.

**Potential correlations to investigate:**

| KORA indicator | Potential correlated outcome |
|---|---|
| Activation Rate | Employee retention rate |
| LIFE pillar strength | Absenteeism, occupational illness incidence |
| GROWTH pillar strength | Internal mobility, promotion rate |
| CONNECTION pillar strength | Engagement survey scores, collaboration indices |
| IMPACT pillar strength | Employer brand metrics, talent attraction measures |
| LEGACY pillar strength | Knowledge retention after departures |
| KORA Index overall | Turnover rate, safety incidents, productivity proxies |

**The essential methodological caution:**

**Correlation is not causation.**

KORA must never claim that a specific action caused a specific organizational outcome unless a rigorous longitudinal study with appropriate controls supports that claim. The outcome correlation layer should be:
- Introduced with transparent methodology and explicit limitations
- Presented as observed statistical associations, not causal claims
- Protected against cherry-picking relationships that appear favorable but lack statistical integrity
- Subject to external academic review before being used in external communications or commercial claims

This layer is a long-term methodological investment. Its value grows with the size and quality of the KORA longitudinal dataset.

## 32. Anti-Gaming Logic

Any scoring system can be gamed. KORA must be designed from the outset to make gaming difficult, detectable and methodologically consequential.

**Gaming patterns KORA must protect against:**

| Pattern | Description |
|---|---|
| Repeated low-value events | High frequency logging of minimal-effort events to accumulate IU without genuine engagement |
| Single-pillar saturation | Concentrating all activity in one pillar to maximize that score while ignoring others |
| Self-declaration inflation | Bulk submission of unverified self-declared actions |
| Excessive provider concentration | Routing all activity through one provider to create appearance of structured programming |
| Quantity without quality | Many short events substituting for fewer deeper engagements |
| Artificial event splitting | Dividing one substantive multi-session engagement into many separately logged micro-events |
| Duplicate records | Submitting the same event multiple times through different data sources |
| One-day event stacking | Logging many distinct event types on a single date in ways that cannot reflect genuine simultaneous participation |
| Unusual spikes | Sudden unexplained large increases in reported events without corresponding operational explanation |
| Missing evidence | Bulk submission of events with no supporting documentation where documentation is expected |

**Anti-gaming controls for Foundation Light v0.1:**

Controls in the first version must be simple, explainable and defensible — not algorithmically opaque.

- **Duplicate detection:** Flag events with identical or near-identical records from overlapping sources
- **Frequency caps:** Apply diminishing AGF to event-type repetition above a threshold within a defined period (e.g., the nth occurrence of the same event type in the same week carries reduced weight)
- **Self-declaration confidence floor:** Self-declared actions (EV multiplier ≤ 0.55) carry lower Impact Confidence and contribute less to the Verification Rate component
- **Single-pillar concentration warning:** Surface a visible warning to the company when one pillar exceeds a concentration threshold (indicative: >60% of Total IU)
- **Source concentration warning:** Surface a warning when >70% of events come from one provider or one source type
- **Unusual spike flag:** Automatically flag for manual review when period-over-period event counts from a single source exceed a threshold increase (indicative: >3× prior period)
- **Incomplete event rejection:** Reject or flag events missing required UEF fields; they do not generate IU until the record is complete or manually reviewed
- **Manual review flag:** Any event or batch that triggers multiple warnings simultaneously is escalated for human review before being counted

Every anti-gaming control must be explainable to a CHRO and defensible to a CTO. Transparency about what is controlled and why builds confidence in the methodology. Opacity in the anti-gaming layer creates distrust.

## 33. Audit Trail & Explainability

KORA must be explainable at every level of output. A company that receives a KORA Index score must be able to understand — and demonstrate to an external reviewer — exactly how that score was produced.

**What must be traceable for every significant KORA output:**

| Traceability element | Description |
|---|---|
| Source | Which system or provider generated the underlying events |
| Event type | What category of action this was |
| Pillar mapping | Which pillar(s) were assigned and why |
| Quality and verification level | CQ and EV values applied |
| Impact Unit calculation | The formula applied with the coefficient values used |
| Formula version | Which version of the IU formula was in effect |
| Impact Confidence | The overall confidence level assigned |
| Eligibility status | The fiscal/budget eligibility classification where applicable |
| Eligibility confidence | The confidence level supporting that classification |

Explainability is not a technical documentation requirement. It is a commercial and ethical commitment. A KORA Index that cannot be explained to a board is a liability. A KORA Index that comes with a complete, traceable narrative is a strategic asset.

## 34. Methodology Versioning

KORA's methodology will evolve. As the platform gathers real data, as expert review deepens, and as calibration improves the formula, the weights, coefficients and rules that produce the KORA Index will change. This is expected and healthy.

But evolution creates a problem: if the formula changes, how can a company compare their KORA Index in year 1 to year 3? How do they know whether an improvement reflects a better program or a different methodology?

**Methodology versioning solves this problem.** Every KORA output carries a methodology version reference identifying exactly which rules, weights and definitions were in effect when the output was produced.

**Components subject to versioning:**

| Component | Versioning reason |
|---|---|
| Impact Unit formula and all coefficients | Core scoring logic — any change must be versioned |
| KORA Index composition and weights | Primary output — historical comparability requires version tracking |
| KORA Contribution formula | Separate indicator — independently versioned |
| KORA Ecosystem Reach formula | Separate indicator — independently versioned |
| Confidence Score model | Affects trustworthiness assessment of all outputs |
| Pillar mapping taxonomy | Changes to pillar classification affect historical comparability |
| Fiscal/budget taxonomy | Country-specific rule changes require versioning |
| Eligibility confidence model | Confidence level hierarchy and triggering events |
| Partner eligibility profile schema | Required fields and validation rules |
| Anti-gaming rules and caps | Changes to AGF logic affect score levels |
| Benchmark normalization parameters | When introduced, changes affect cross-company comparability |
| Privacy threshold definitions | Safe aggregation thresholds and sensitivity classifications |
| Policy rule framework | Configurable rule structure and enforcement logic |

**The historical record:** When KORA advances from v0.1 to v0.2, the prior version's documentation must be retained and accessible. A score produced under v0.1 must remain interpretable under v0.1's rules, regardless of what has changed since. This is essential for trust, auditability, and the Certified tier — where an external reviewer must reconstruct and validate a historical score.

## 35. Human Review / Advisor Governance Layer

KORA is technology-powered. It is not fully automated. In ambiguous, complex or high-stakes situations, human judgment must govern.

**Situations requiring human review or advisor involvement:**
- Events where pillar mapping is ambiguous and automated classification could reasonably assign to multiple pillars
- Events with unusual magnitude that may reflect genuine rare engagements or data errors
- Eligibility classifications that are Conditional or Uncertain where tax-advantaged activation is planned
- Anti-gaming flags triggered by patterns that may reflect genuine unusual activity or manipulation
- First-time event types not previously seen in the KORA taxonomy
- Disputes between a company and KORA's automated classification of their data
- Any override of an automated output — overrides must be logged with reviewer identity and rationale
- Certified tier assessments — always require external KORA-authorized advisor review

**The governing principle:**

**The algorithm proposes. The governance layer controls.**

KORA's methodology is a framework for structured judgment, not a machine that replaces judgment. When the framework produces an output a qualified human would question, the qualified human must be able to intervene — and the intervention must be recorded in the audit trail.

This positions KORA as technology-powered and trust-governed — which is exactly what is required for credibility with boards, investors, regulators and advisors.

## 36. AI Boundary

Artificial intelligence tools may support the KORA methodology. They must not become the KORA methodology.

**AI may support:**
- Event classification during data ingestion — suggesting a pillar mapping that a human confirms
- Column mapping during data normalization — identifying which fields in a raw export correspond to UEF fields
- Anomaly detection — flagging events that appear unusual relative to historical patterns
- Report narrative drafting — generating text descriptions of what data shows, subject to human review
- Insight generation — surfacing patterns and relationships for human evaluation
- Data quality warnings — identifying likely errors, duplicates or inconsistencies
- Fiscal eligibility pre-tagging — generating a preliminary KORA-Inferred classification as a starting point for advisor review
- Partner service classification suggestions — proposing pillar mappings for new partner services, subject to review
- Pattern detection — identifying workforce activation trends across periods

**AI must NOT:**
- Become an opaque scoring authority whose outputs cannot be explained or reconstructed
- Assign discretionary Impact Unit weights without rule-based justification traceable in the audit trail
- Hide uncertainty behind confident-looking outputs
- Replace the audit trail required for Certified assessments
- Make automated individual decisions about employees that are visible to or actionable by employers
- Produce unexplained rankings of individuals or groups
- Override the privacy architecture or safe aggregation thresholds
- Determine fiscal eligibility as a final, authoritative judgment
- Substitute for qualified advisor review in Conditional or Uncertain eligibility cases

Every KORA score must remain explainable, auditable and rule-based. Where AI proposes a classification or flag, a human must be able to understand why, agree or disagree, and record the outcome. AI is an efficiency tool and pattern-detection instrument — an assistant to the KORA methodology, not an authority over it.

## 37. Data Quality & Ingestion Reliability

KORA often receives imperfect data. Companies maintain records in systems not designed with KORA in mind. Field names vary, coverage is uneven, time periods do not align, and some sources are rich while others are sparse. KORA must handle this reality honestly.

**Data quality dimensions evaluated at ingestion:**

| Dimension | Description |
|---|---|
| Missing required fields | Which UEF fields are absent in this data source? |
| Duplicate records | Same event appearing multiple times across sources |
| Inconsistent sources | Two sources reporting conflicting records for the same event |
| Low-confidence data | Events present but insufficient evidence for confident classification |
| Rejected events | Records that cannot be processed; reason logged |
| Source completeness | What proportion of expected data from this source was received? |
| Pillar-level data gaps | One or more pillars essentially undocumented |
| Outdated data | Records from periods outside the stated analysis window |
| Inconsistent time periods | Welfare data covering Q1–Q3 while training data covers Q2–Q4 |
| Weak evidence references | Events logged without supporting documentation where documentation is expected |
| Missing fiscal/budget tags | Initiatives without a budget category assigned |
| Outdated eligibility classifications | Fiscal classifications flagged as Outdated — Requires Review |
| Incomplete partner eligibility profiles | Partner services missing required eligibility profile fields |

**What KORA shows to the company:**
- Data completeness score per source and per pillar
- Specific data quality warnings with plain-language explanations
- Confidence level per pillar based on available evidence
- Rejected or excluded records with reason codes
- Explicit list of assumptions used where data was insufficient
- Fields that, if provided, would meaningfully improve confidence and the KORA Index

**The founding principle of data quality:**

**A low-quality dataset produces lower confidence, not fake precision.**

KORA must not compensate for poor data quality by generating confident-looking scores from weak inputs. If the data is sparse, self-declared or poorly evidenced, the Confidence Score must reflect this and the output must say so. A Foundation Light report that honestly describes its data limitations and recommends what the company should collect next is more valuable than one that presents false precision.

## 38. Confidence Score

The Confidence Score is KORA's summary indicator of the overall methodological reliability of a company's KORA Index and related outputs. It tells users how much trust to place in the score as a whole — separate from the score itself.

**The Confidence Score may combine:**

| Input | Description |
|---|---|
| Data completeness | What proportion of required UEF fields are populated across all events? |
| Source reliability | What proportion of events come from high-reliability source types? |
| Verification coverage | What is the company's Verification Rate (from Section 19)? |
| Privacy-safe aggregation quality | Are all employer-visible outputs above safe aggregation thresholds? |
| Missing fields rate | What proportion of events have one or more missing quality-relevant fields? |
| Low-confidence data share | What proportion of Total IU comes from the two lowest confidence source types? |
| Manual review rate | What proportion of events required manual review or override? |
| Eligibility confidence relevance | For companies using tax-advantaged perimeters, what proportion of activations carry Advisor-Confirmed or Partner-Documented eligibility confidence? |

**Confidence Score is not the same as the KORA Index.** A company may achieve a moderate KORA Index with a high Confidence Score — meaning the score is reliable even if not excellent — which is more useful than a high KORA Index with a low Confidence Score, where the output cannot be trusted.

The Confidence Score should be prominently displayed alongside the KORA Index in all executive outputs. Every report should make clear: this is the score, and this is how much to trust it.

## 39. Legal and Ethical Guardrails

*Conceptual only. Not legal advice.*

KORA must be designed to reduce legal and ethical risk by default — not to add compliance as a retrofit after the fact.

**Structural commitments:**
- Minimize personal data collected and processed to what is analytically necessary
- Protect sensitive data categories beyond standard anonymization
- Prevent employer-visible individual scoring from existing in the platform architecture
- Avoid any logic interpretable as automated individual evaluation under GDPR Article 22
- Maintain full auditability of scoring and classification decisions
- Separate employer-visible aggregate intelligence from protected individual records at the architecture level

**Commercial integrity commitments:**
- Mark all Foundation Light outputs as pre-calibration and provisional until validated
- Do not sell the Certified tier before the methodology is mature, published and externally reviewable
- Do not use client data for marketing claims without explicit consent
- Do not present KORA outputs as scientifically definitive when they are provisional
- Do not become a payment or financial intermediary by accident — keep KORA revenue structurally separate from company service funds

**Trust commitments:**
- Make uncertainty visible in every output
- Allow companies to understand the basis of every score they receive
- Design the platform so that workers are not harmed by its outputs
- Design the platform so that advisors can independently review and validate its methodology
- Design the platform to become more trustworthy over time as data matures, not less

KORA should be trusted because it is transparent about what it knows, what it does not know, and how every output was produced. Transparency is not a risk for KORA. Opacity is.

## 41. Methodological Status & Open Assumptions

Every Foundation Light report and dashboard output must carry the notation:

> *Produced under KORA Methodology v0.1 — Pre-Calibration. All weights and coefficients are provisional. Outputs reflect structured analytical judgment, not statistically validated parameters.*

**Validation path for open assumptions:**

4. **Statistical calibration** once minimum dataset thresholds are met across multiple companies
5. **Sensitivity analysis** on key formula parameters — understanding which assumptions most affect the KORA Index
6. **Advisor methodology review** for fiscal eligibility and policy rule logic — qualified labor law and tax specialists
7. **Academic partnership** for external peer review and publication of the methodology
8. **Multi-company benchmarking** as the KORA network grows — enabling sector and size normalization
9. **Annual methodology review cycle** — formal versioned review of all provisional parameters
10. **Legal/tax advisor review** for fiscal eligibility taxonomy by jurisdiction — as KORA expands internationally

KORA's credibility grows as each of these validation steps is completed. The foundation is a rigorous, honest, documented methodology — not a claim of scientific certainty that cannot yet be supported. Acknowledging what is provisional is not weakness. It is the intellectual integrity that makes the system trustworthy.
